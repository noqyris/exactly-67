Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 October 2026 (version 1.3.1)

Exactly 67 ("the app") is a number puzzle game. There are no accounts and no sign-in, and we, the developer, run no servers: nothing you do in the game is sent to us. The app does show ads, and the ad service it uses collects some data from your device. This policy explains what, why, and how you control it.

Advertising

Unless you have bought an ad-free option (see In-app purchases), the app shows a banner ad at the bottom of the screen, an occasional full-screen ad between levels, and short videos you can choose to watch for a reward, such as extra hints or a streak freeze. Each video names its reward before you watch it.

These ads are provided by Unity LevelPlay, an ad mediation service run by Unity Technologies (formerly ironSource), with ads from Unity Ads and ironSource Exchange (both Unity Technologies). The ad software is part of the app, but it sends data directly to Unity, not to us, and Unity handles that data under its own privacy policy.

What Unity and its ad partners may collect

  • Your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you allow tracking when iOS asks. If you don't, iOS gives the app a blank identifier instead.
  • The identifier for vendor (IDFV), an identifier iOS gives each developer's apps on your device.
  • Your IP address.
  • Device and system information, such as device model, operating system version, language and connection type.
  • Ad interaction data, such as which ads were shown, whether a video was watched to the end, and taps on ads.
  • Performance and diagnostic data about the ad software, such as how long ads take to load and crash reports, which Unity uses to check ad quality.

What it is used for

  • Choosing and showing ads, and limiting how often the same ad appears.
  • Measuring how ads perform and which ads lead to an app install.
  • Personalising ads, only with your consent and, for the advertising identifier, your tracking permission.
  • Detecting fraud and invalid ad activity.

Your choices

The app asks nothing about ads when you open it, and the ad software does not start. The first time you finish a level, it asks whether you agree to ads: Exactly 67 (Noqyris) shows ads through Unity LevelPlay; if you accept, Unity Technologies (Unity Ads and ironSource Exchange) receives device and app data, which ads you see and tap and, only if you also agree to tracking, your advertising identifier, to show you ads and tailor them to you. Both answers, Accept and Don't accept, are offered the same way. If you haven't answered, the question comes again after a later level, or when you tap an offer to watch an ad for a reward.

  • If you accept, iOS then asks whether the app may track you (App Tracking Transparency), and only then does the ad software start. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, ads are still shown without your advertising identifier; you can change this in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • If you don't accept, the ad software never starts: it collects nothing, and no ads are shown, including reward videos. Everything those videos give can still be earned by playing — hints come from the daily gift, the Star Jar, pack bonuses and streak milestones, and hints buy streak freezes and repairs. The app does not ask again by itself: offers to watch an ad are hidden for 180 days, after which tapping one asks once more.

To change your answer at any time, open Settings (the gear on the main menu) → Privacy & ads; the same link is in the top corner of the main menu. If you withdraw after ads have started, the app removes the banner at once, stops full-screen ads and reward videos, stops loading ads and tells the ad service not to sell or share your personal information; from the next start the ad software is not started at all. Privacy & ads is not shown if you own Unlimited hints, because the ad software never runs for you.

Report an ad: Settings → Report an ad opens your mail app with a message to us that names the app version, so you can tell us about an ad that is inappropriate or not suitable for your age. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

Many Unity ads also show a Data Privacy (shield) icon that you can tap to opt out of certain personalised ads. For more on how Unity uses this data and the choices you have, read the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy and Unity's Cookie & Advertising Policy. To make a privacy request about ad data, contact Unity as described in its policy: we don't hold that data.

In-app purchases

All purchases are optional:

  • Hint packs of 10, 30 or 100 hints. Each pack can be bought as many times as you like, and bought hints never expire. Your hint balance is stored on your device, so it is lost if you delete the app.
  • Welcome pack, 25 hints, a one-time offer for players who have not bought anything in the app on this device. It disappears once you buy it or anything else. While it is offered, it takes the place of the 10-hint pack in the Store. Its hints go into your hint balance like any other hints.
  • No ads, a one-time purchase. It removes the banner and the full-screen ads between levels. Hints are not included. The reward videos stay available if you choose to watch one, so the ad software still runs (if you have given consent).
  • Unlimited hints, a one-time purchase. It gives you unlimited hints and no ads. With it, the app does not start the ad software at all.

Purchases are processed by Apple (on Android, by Google Play). The app never sees your payment details, name or Apple ID, and we receive no personal information about you from a purchase. What you own, and whether you have bought anything, is remembered on your device. Restore purchases in the Store asks Apple to re-apply No ads or Unlimited hints, for example after you reinstall. Hints you bought, including the Welcome pack, are part of your hint balance and are not restored.

Daily reminders

The app can remind you when a new Daily Challenge is ready. Reminders are off unless you turn them on: the app offers them on the win card after some puzzles (Remind me daily, offered a few times at most), and there is a Daily reminder switch in the game's Settings. Only when you tap one of these does iOS ask whether the app may send you notifications, and no reminder is scheduled unless you allow it.

Reminders are local notifications: the app schedules them on your device, and iOS shows them at the planned time. There is no push server, and nothing about your reminders is sent to us or to anyone else. The app writes each reminder from what it already stores on your device, such as your Daily streak, your daily gift and the level you are on. To time them, it keeps the times of your last few app launches (up to seven) on your device and aims for the time of day you usually play. Reminders are planned only for between 9:00 and 21:00 your time, contain no ads or prices, and stop after a month if you don't come back. If you open the app by tapping a reminder, the app notes on your device which screen to show, and deletes that note as soon as it starts.

To turn reminders off, switch off Daily reminder in the game's Settings, which cancels any that are scheduled, or turn off notifications for Exactly 67 in iOS Settings → Notifications.

Data stored on your device

The app saves the following only on your device, using the operating system's standard app storage:

  • Your level progress (stars earned and the fewest pieces you used per level, from which your pack medals are worked out) and the date you last finished the Daily Challenge.
  • Your Daily Challenge history: which days' puzzles you solved (about the last two months, for the calendar), the trophy you earned each month, and your Daily streak (its current and best length and the dates needed to keep count, the streak freezes you hold, and a broken streak you can still repair).
  • Your hint balance, the stars in your Star Jar, and which packs' one-time completion bonus you have already received.
  • The day you last claimed the daily gift and how many days in a row you have claimed it. If you updated from an earlier version, the date of the last free daily hint it gave you also stays on your device, but it is not used after the update.
  • How many reward videos you have watched today for each kind of reward, so the daily limits can be applied.
  • Your sound, music, haptics and daily reminder settings.
  • Which purchases you own, whether you have ever bought anything, and whether you bought the Welcome pack.
  • A count of recent level clears, used to space out full-screen ads, and whether the app has already asked you to rate it.
  • How many full-screen ads you have seen, how many times you have opened the app, and how often and when the app last suggested No ads, so that suggestion stays rare.
  • How many times, and when, the app last offered daily reminders, and the times of your last few app launches, used to time reminders (see Daily reminders).
  • Your ad consent choice, which the ad software stores so it isn't asked again every launch; when the app last asked for your ad consent and how many times the question has been answered each way (counts only, with no date or identifier), so that a no is not asked again for 180 days; and a flag recording that the app has already cleared, once, any consent answer left over from an earlier version of the app.

None of this is sent to us. It is removed if you delete the app. Like other app data, it is included in your device's backups if you use them (for example iCloud Backup).

Ratings and sharing

After you clear a level, the app may ask Apple to show its standard rating prompt, once. Apple handles the prompt and any rating you leave. The Share button on the win card opens your device's share sheet with a short text summary of how you solved the level. It contains no personal data, and where you send it is up to you.

Network

The puzzles themselves work fully offline. The app uses the network only to load ads (after you have given consent), to make or restore purchases through Apple, and for Apple's rating prompt. Daily reminders are scheduled on your device and use no network.

Children

Exactly 67 is a general-audience game. It is not directed at children under 13, and it tells the ad software that it is not a child-directed app. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child's information has been collected through the ads in this app, contact us at the address below.
